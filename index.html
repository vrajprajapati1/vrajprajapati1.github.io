About me

When I was 14, I stood underneath a full-size Saturn V at the Kennedy Space Center with my family. What got me wasn't just the scale—it was seeing all the mechanical linkages, valves, and machined hardware that went into making it fly. That moment decided my path: I want to pursue graduate school in astronautical engineering and help build hardware that flies to Mars.

My foundation started on the shop floor at the Dos Pueblos Engineering Academy, continued through Santa Barbara City College's rocketry club, and brought me to Cal Poly Pomona, where I'm currently a junior contributing to propulsion hardware with THRUST. I'm still early in my engineering career, and I love being in environments where I can ask questions, learn real manufacturing tolerances, and turn CAD models into working parts.

Stepping up to a manual lathe or vertical mill puts me in a peaceful trance—dialing in a cut and watching chips curl is my favorite kind of focus. When something breaks, I enjoy taking it apart to trace the root cause and machine the fix. My dad taught me early on to take pride in doing honest, thorough work, and I carry that mindset into every build.
