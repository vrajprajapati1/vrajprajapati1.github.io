About me

When I was 14, I stood underneath a full-size Saturn V at the Kennedy Space Center with my family. What hooked me wasn’t just the scale—it was seeing all the mechanical linkages, valves, and precision-machined hardware that went into making it fly. That moment set my direction: I want to pursue graduate school in astronautical engineering and help design hardware that flies to Mars.

My engineering foundation started on the shop floor at the Dos Pueblos Engineering Academy, continued through Santa Barbara City College’s rocket club, and brought me to Cal Poly Pomona, where I’m currently a junior contributing to propulsion hardware with THRUST. Being early in my career, I thrive in hands-on shops and labs where I can ask questions, learn real manufacturing tolerances, and turn CAD models into working parts.

Stepping up to a manual lathe or vertical mill puts me in a peaceful trance—dialing in a cut and watching chips curl is my favorite kind of focus. When a mechanism jams or runs backward, I enjoy tearing it down to trace the root cause and machine the fix. My dad taught me early on to take pride in doing honest, thorough work, and I carry that mindset into every build.
