About me

When I was 14, I stood underneath a full-size Saturn V at the Kennedy Space Center with my family[cite: 3]. What got me wasn't just the size—it was seeing all the mechanical linkages, valves, and linkages that went into making it fly[cite: 3]. That moment decided my path: I want to pursue graduate school in astronautical engineering and help build hardware that flies to Mars[cite: 3, 10].

My foundation started on the shop floor at the Dos Pueblos Engineering Academy, continued through Santa Barbara City College's rocketry club, and brought me to Cal Poly Pomona, where I'm currently a junior contributing to propulsion hardware with THRUST[cite: 1, 3, 10]. I'm still early in my engineering career, and I love being in environments where I can ask questions, learn real manufacturing tolerances, and turn CAD models into working parts.

Stepping up to a manual lathe or vertical mill honestly puts me in a trance—dialing in a cut and watching chips curl is my peace time. When something breaks, I enjoy taking it apart to trace the root cause and machine the fix[cite: 3]. My dad taught me early on to take pride in doing honest, thorough work—a mindset I carry into every build.
